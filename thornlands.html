Selling in Thornlands · QLD 4164

Your Thornlands questions, answered

I work Thornlands every week, from the townhouse complexes off Freshwater Street to family homes across the suburb. These are the questions Thornlands owners ask me most, answered plainly.

What is my Thornlands home worth?

Whatever buyers have recently paid for homes like yours, close to yours. In Thornlands that can change a lot from one estate or complex to the next, so a suburb median is a poor guide for a single home. I'll show you the recent sales that genuinely compare, on your street first and then nearby, and give you a figure you can check against them.

How long does it take to sell in Thornlands?

It depends mostly on the price, the method of sale and the time of year. At the appraisal I'll show you how long comparable Thornlands homes took to sell recently, so you're working from local numbers rather than a national headline. The settlement date is agreed in the contract, so you can line it up with your next move.

What does it cost to sell?

The main costs are the agent's commission, the marketing (photography, the portal listings, the signboard) and your solicitor or conveyancer. Before I start, the commission and every marketing cost are written into the agent appointment form (Form 6) for you to sign. You know the numbers before anything is spent.

Should I sell by auction or private sale?

Both are used here, and the right one depends on your home and your plans. At auction, the winning bidder signs an unconditional contract on the day and there is no cooling-off period. In a private sale, buyers can make their offer subject to conditions such as finance and a building and pest inspection, and they get a 5 business day cooling-off period. I'll lay out both with recent local results so you can choose.

What paperwork do I need before I can sell?

Since 1 August 2025, Queensland sellers must give buyers a signed seller disclosure statement (Form 2) and the prescribed certificates for the property before the buyer signs a contract. Your solicitor or conveyancer normally prepares these, and for a townhouse or unit in a body corporate that includes the body corporate certificate. Engage them early so the documents are ready the day your home goes to market.

Can a buyer pull out after signing?

In a private sale, yes, during the cooling-off period. Buyers get 5 business days from when they receive the contract signed by both parties. If they cancel in that time, the seller can keep up to 0.25% of the price from the deposit. There is no cooling-off period at auction, and sellers don't get one. A buyer can also end the contract if a condition, such as finance, isn't met by its due date.

Should I sell first or buy first?

Neither is right for everyone. Selling first gives you a firm budget. Buying first means you know where you're going. Some sellers agree a longer settlement to bridge the gap. Talk it through with your lender as well as with me.

My property is tenanted. Can I still sell?

Yes. Selling doesn't end the lease, and tenants are entitled to proper written notice before each inspection. I work out inspection times with your property manager, and we can sell to investors with the lease in place or plan the sale around the lease ending.
